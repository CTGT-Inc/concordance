# Tallowmere Public Library District: Branch Operations Manual

Edition 4. Synthetic document written for the Concordance toy pipeline. The
organisation, its branches, its staff roles and every rule below are invented.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This manual sets out the operating rules for the eleven branches, the two
mobile library vehicles and the central stores depot of the Tallowmere Public
Library District. It applies to all permanent staff, temporary staff,
volunteers and contractors working on District premises or with District
collections.

The manual describes what staff do in routine situations. It does not replace
the District's employment handbook, the facilities contract or the board's
adopted policies, which take precedence where they differ. Branch managers may
issue local instructions that add detail to this manual, but a local
instruction may not relax a rule stated here.

Sections 5, 6 and 12 deal with the handling of materials and money and are the
sections most often consulted at the service desk. Section 9 explains what to
do when two rules in this manual point in different directions.

## 2. Definitions

In this manual, a patron is any person holding a current District library card
or applying for one. A household account is a set of patron records sharing one
postal address and one responsible adult. A branch manager is the person
appointed to run a branch, or the deputy acting in that role for the day.

The duty officer is the senior staff member on the floor during opening hours.
At small branches the branch manager is usually also the duty officer. The
operations desk is the District's central support team, reachable by internal
line during all opening hours and by the on-call phone outside them.

Material means any item the District lends, including printed books, audio and
video media, tools from the lending shelf, study kits and loanable laptops. A
hold is a request by a patron to borrow an item that is currently on loan or
held at another branch.

## 3. Branch opening hours and closures

Each branch opens on the hours published on the District website and posted at
its entrance. Staff must arrive at least fifteen minutes before opening to
complete the opening checklist, which covers the alarm panel, the public
computer start-up and a walk-through of all public rooms.

A branch may close early only with the approval of the operations desk. The
duty officer must record the reason for any unplanned closure in the daily log
and post a closure notice on the entrance door before the public is asked to
leave.

Planned closures for staff training or maintenance must be announced on the
website and at the branch at least fourteen days in advance. When a branch is
closed for more than two consecutive opening days, items on hold at that branch
are moved to the nearest open branch and patrons are notified by their chosen
contact method.

Weather closures are decided by the operations desk for all branches at once
and are not decided branch by branch.

## 4. Patron registration and cards

A person may register for a library card in person at any branch or through the
online form. In-person applicants must show a document bearing their name and a
current address within the District service area. Online applicants receive a
provisional card that allows holds and digital loans only, until they complete
the address check at a branch.

Children under the age of thirteen are registered on a household account with
a responsible adult, who must sign the registration form. Staff must not
register a child under thirteen without a responsible adult present or a
completed form signed in advance.

A replacement card costs the amount shown in the District fee schedule. The
first replacement in any twelve-month period is free. Staff may not issue a
second active card on one patron record; the old card number must be cancelled
when a replacement is issued.

Patrons who live outside the service area may hold a visitor card, which allows
up to five items on loan at a time and no interlibrary requests.

## 5. Loans, renewals and holds

Loan periods and renewal limits depend on the material type and are set out in
Table 1. The loan period starts on the day of checkout. Items are due back by
closing time on the due date at any branch, or by midnight in a book return
slot.

**Table 1. Loan periods by material type**

| Material type        | Loan period | Renewals allowed | Holds allowed | Daily overdue fine |
|----------------------|-------------|------------------|---------------|--------------------|
| Printed book (adult) | 21 days     | 2                | Yes           | 0.20               |
| Printed book (child) | 21 days     | 2                | Yes           | none               |
| Audio and video      | 14 days     | 1                | Yes           | 0.50               |
| Study kit            | 14 days     | 0                | Yes           | 1.00               |
| Lending-shelf tool   | 7 days      | 0                | No            | 2.00               |
| Loanable laptop      | 7 days      | 1                | No            | 5.00               |

An item may not be renewed if another patron has a hold on it. Renewal extends
the loan by one full loan period from the date of renewal, not from the
original due date.

A patron may have up to fifteen holds active at once. When a held item becomes
available, the patron has seven days to collect it. Uncollected holds are
released to the next patron in the queue, and a patron who fails to collect
three holds in a six-month period may have hold privileges suspended for thirty
days.

Loanable laptops and lending-shelf tools may only be borrowed by patrons aged
eighteen or over who have held a card for at least ninety days.

## 6. Overdue materials, fines and fee waivers

Overdue fines accrue at the daily rate shown in Table 1 for each day the
branch is open after the due date. Days on which the item's home branch is
closed are not counted. Fines on a single item stop accruing when they reach
the replacement cost of the item.

A patron account is blocked from new loans when the outstanding balance
exceeds 10.00. A blocked account may still place holds and use public
computers. The block lifts automatically when the balance falls to 10.00 or
less.

Items more than forty-five days overdue are treated as lost under section 12,
and the replacement cost is added to the account in place of further fines.

Staff may waive fines in the following cases: the patron was in hospital or
bereaved during the loan period; the item was returned to a slot that was out
of service; or the patron is a child and the fine relates to a child's item
that does not normally carry a fine. Staff may waive up to 25.00 per account
per year on their own authority. Waivers above 25.00 require the branch
manager's approval.

Each waiver must be recorded in the account notes with the reason and the
initials of the staff member who granted it.

## 7. Interlibrary requests

Patrons with a full card may request items the District does not own through
the regional interlibrary scheme. Each patron may have up to four interlibrary
requests open at once.

The lending library sets the loan period for an interlibrary item, and staff
must not extend it without the lending library's written agreement. Interlibrary
items may not be renewed through the self-service kiosk.

A handling charge of 3.00 applies to each interlibrary request that is filled.
The charge is not refunded if the patron does not collect the item. Requests
for items published in the last six months are declined automatically, because
the scheme does not lend new titles.

Damage to an interlibrary item is reported to the lending library within two
working days, and the patron is charged the amount the lending library invoices.

## 8. Public computers and network use

Public computers are available to anyone, including people without a library
card, through a guest session. Card holders may book up to two hours per day
in advance. Guest sessions are limited to thirty minutes and cannot be booked.

Staff must not install software on public computers or change their settings.
Faults are reported to the operations desk through the fault form, and the
computer is marked out of service until the operations desk clears it.

Users must not use District computers or the public wireless network to access
material that is unlawful. Staff who observe such use must end the session and
inform the duty officer, who decides whether to file an incident report under
section 10.

Printing is charged per page at the rate in the fee schedule. Print jobs not
released within one hour are deleted.

## 9. Conflict resolution between rules

Several rules in this manual can apply to the same situation and point in
different directions. The rules in this section say which one prevails. Each
one names the two rules it reconciles, the action staff take and who the case
is escalated to. Where a situation is not covered here, staff apply the more
cautious of the two rules and refer the case to the branch manager.

### 9.1 Account block and fee waiver

When the account block in section 6 and a fee waiver under section 6 both apply
to the same account, staff must apply the waiver first and then check the
balance again. If the balance still exceeds the block threshold after the
waiver, staff must leave the block in place and escalate to the branch manager.

### 9.2 Hold collection and branch closure

When the seven-day collection period for a hold and an unplanned branch closure
under section 3 both apply to the same item, staff must extend the collection
period by the number of days the branch was closed. If the extension would
exceed fourteen days in total, staff must escalate to the operations desk.

### 9.3 Renewal limit and interlibrary loan period

When the renewal limits in Table 1 and the lending library's loan period under
section 7 both apply to the same item, staff must follow the lending library's
loan period. If the patron asks for more time than the lending library allows,
staff must escalate to the interlibrary coordinator.

### 9.4 Visitor card limit and household account

When the five-item visitor card limit in section 4 and a household account
under section 4 both apply to the same patron, staff must apply the visitor card
limit to that patron's own loans. If the responsible adult asks for the limit to
be pooled across the household, staff must escalate to the branch manager.

### 9.5 Lost-item charge and overdue fine

When the lost-item charge under section 12 and the overdue fine under section 6
both apply to the same item, staff must remove the accrued fine and keep only
the replacement cost. If the patron disputes the replacement cost, staff must
escalate to the branch manager.

### 9.6 Age restriction and household borrowing

When the age restriction on laptops and tools in section 5 and a household
account under section 4 both apply to the same loan, staff must issue the item
only on the responsible adult's own card. If the responsible adult asks for the
item to be issued on a child's card, staff must escalate to the duty officer.

## 10. Incident reporting

Any event that causes injury, damage, a threat to safety, or a serious
disruption to service is an incident. Incidents are classified by severity as
set out in Table 2, and the severity decides how quickly the incident is
reported and who must be told.

**Table 2. Incident severity levels**

| Level | Description                                        | Report within    | Notify                         |
|-------|----------------------------------------------------|------------------|--------------------------------|
| 1     | Minor disruption, no injury, no damage             | End of day       | Branch manager                 |
| 2     | Minor injury or damage under 500.00                | 4 hours          | Branch manager, operations desk|
| 3     | Injury needing treatment, or damage over 500.00    | 1 hour           | Operations desk, facilities    |
| 4     | Serious injury, threat to life, or building evacuated | Immediately   | Emergency services, operations desk, district director |

The duty officer must complete the incident form for every incident at level 2
or above. Level 1 incidents are recorded in the daily log only.

Staff must not discuss an incident with the press or on social media. Enquiries
from the press are passed to the district communications officer.

Where the severity is uncertain, staff must report at the higher of the two
levels being considered. A level may be lowered later by the operations desk
but not by branch staff.

## 11. Building safety and evacuation

Each branch has a named fire warden for every opening session. The fire warden
must check that all fire exits are clear at opening and after any delivery.

When the fire alarm sounds, staff must stop service, direct everyone to the
nearest exit and go to the assembly point shown on the branch floor plan. The
fire warden must sweep the public rooms and the staff areas before leaving the
building, unless doing so would put the warden at risk.

No one may re-enter the building until the fire service or the operations desk
gives permission. An evacuation is always reported as a level 4 incident under
Table 2, even if it turns out to be a false alarm.

Fire drills are held twice a year at every branch, on dates set by the
operations desk. The result of each drill, including the time taken to clear
the building, is recorded in the branch safety file.

## 12. Handling of lost and damaged items

An item is treated as lost when the patron reports it lost or when it is more
than forty-five days overdue. The replacement cost recorded in the catalogue is
added to the patron's account. If the item is returned within six months of
being declared lost, the replacement cost is refunded and any fine for the
overdue period is reinstated up to the cap in section 6.

Staff must inspect every returned item for damage at check-in. Damaged items
are set aside for the branch's collections officer, who decides whether the
item is repaired, replaced or withdrawn. The patron is charged for damage
only when the collections officer records that the damage occurred during the
patron's loan.

Lending-shelf tools must be checked against their parts list at check-in.
Missing parts are charged at the cost listed on the tool's catalogue record.

## 13. Records retention and patron privacy

Loan history is kept only for as long as an item is on loan and for thirty days
after it is returned, unless the patron has opted in to keeping a reading
history. Staff must not look up a patron's loan history except to resolve a
query from that patron or to recover a District item.

Requests from any outside body for patron records are passed to the district
director without reply. Staff must not confirm or deny whether a person holds a
library card to anyone other than that person or, for a child, the responsible
adult on the household account.

Daily logs and incident forms are kept for six years. Public computer booking
records are deleted after ninety days.

## 14. Review and amendment of this manual

The operations desk reviews this manual every two years, or sooner if the board
adopts a policy that changes a rule in it. Proposed changes are circulated to
branch managers for comment for at least twenty-one days before adoption.

Each edition replaces the previous one in full. Branch managers must remove
printed copies of the previous edition from staff areas within seven days of a
new edition taking effect.

Questions about the meaning of a rule in this manual are sent to the operations
desk, which keeps a register of its answers and includes them in the next
edition where they change practice.
